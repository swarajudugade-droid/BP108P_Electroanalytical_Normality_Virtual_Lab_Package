Experiment setup

Conductance is simulated as a V-shaped curve: high initially, falling as H⁺ is replaced, then rising as excess OH⁻ appears.

Aim & principle

Determine the normality of an unknown strong acid by titration with standard sodium hydroxide while recording conductance after successive additions.

NₐVₐ = NᵦVᵦ at equivalence

Estimate the endpoint from the intersection of the two approximately linear regions of the conductance curve.

Experiment setup

Illustrative mixture: the first endpoint corresponds to neutralization of the strong acid; the second to total acid neutralization.

Aim & principle

Use changes in conductance to identify two endpoints in a mixture of strong and weak acids titrated with standard NaOH.

N(strong acid)V(sample) ≈ N(NaOH)V₁
N(weak acid)V(sample) ≈ N(NaOH)(V₂ − V₁)

This simplified relation assumes one equivalent per mole for each monoprotic acid and a clear two-endpoint curve.

Experiment setup

The simulator calculates idealized pH for a strong acid–strong base titration at 25 °C and displays pH against NaOH volume.

Aim & principle

Determine unknown strong-acid normality by monitoring potential (pH) during addition of standard NaOH. The endpoint is near the steepest portion of the titration curve.

NₐVₐ = NᵦVᵦ
pH = −log₁₀[H⁺] (acidic region)

A glass electrode measures potential related to hydrogen-ion activity. Calibrate the pH meter using suitable buffers before practical use.

Post-test

Practical record checklist

Record aim, apparatus, chemicals, procedure, observations, graph, calculation, result, precautions and instructor signature according to your institution's manual.

  • Rinse and calibrate the conductivity cell / pH electrode as appropriate.
  • Keep temperature as constant as practical; conductivity is temperature-sensitive.
  • Add titrant in smaller increments near endpoint and mix consistently.
  • Use replicate readings and document actual standard titrant concentration.
  • Dispose of solutions according to the laboratory SOP.

Calculation guide

Normality: at equivalence, equivalents of analyte = equivalents of titrant.

N(unknown) = [N(standard) × V(endpoint)] / V(sample)

For the mixed-acid conductometric experiment, if the first endpoint is attributable to the strong acid and the second to total acid, use V₁ for strong-acid equivalents and (V₂ − V₁) for weak-acid equivalents. Confirm this assignment from the prescribed method and actual curve.
